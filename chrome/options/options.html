            Research

            Miru makes claims about attention. Here is what they rest on, so you can check them rather than take them.

            Every design decision below was made against published work, not intuition. Where the evidence said something uncomfortable — that the part people most want is the part that changes them least — Miru followed the evidence.

            A dial at the door works, and the effect is not small

            In a six-week controlled study, 58 students used a prompt that asked their purpose on arrival and reminded them of it a few minutes in. Daily time on the site fell from 27 minutes to 15, daily visits from 29 to 11, scrolling by 42% — and some of it held after the tool was removed, because people had built the habit of asking themselves what they came for.

            Lyngs et al., “I Just Want to Hack Myself to Not Get Distracted”, CHI 2020 · read it

            In Miru: the stay dial. You name a length at the door of a calmed place, and it opens at zero every time so the length is chosen rather than repeated.

            The same study shows how such a prompt becomes hated

            Its banner grew larger until dismissed, it treated a twenty-second visit like an hour of scrolling, and it demanded a justification. Participants described clicking out of it and staying on the site out of spite. The author's own advice: longer intervals, less invasive presentation, and framing that supports the person's goals instead of imposing someone else's.

            Lyngs et al., CHI 2020, participant interviews

            In Miru: nothing grows, nothing repeats within a visit, and nothing asks you to justify yourself. The dial offers two real doors — stay, or leave.

            Statistics alone raise awareness and rarely change behaviour

            Screen-time tracking reliably improves self-awareness but seldom reduces use. People prefer informational tracking to blocking or nudging while rating it less effective — most strongly among those most dependent on the site. Facebook's own time-monitoring features showed no association with lower depression, anxiety or stress.

            Journal of the Association for Consumer Research, 2021 · read it — and JMIR, 2022 · read it

            In Miru: “Looking back” exists, but it is not the feature. The dial is. The record is there to be consulted, not to do the work on its own.

            A repeated warning stops being seen at the second showing

            Brain imaging shows a sharp drop in visual processing after only the second exposure to a warning, falling further each time. Warnings whose appearance varies resisted this: over a three-week field experiment, adherence to fixed warnings decayed while the varying ones held.

            Anderson et al., CHI 2015 · read it — and Vance et al., MIS Quarterly 2018

            In Miru: the breath rotates its words, and the line above the dial changes its phrasing each time it appears.

            Being shown the gap makes people defensive

            One study is named after a participant: “In my defense, only three hours on Instagram.” Confronted with the distance between what they assumed and what they did, people build a case rather than absorb it. Framing that distance as a failure produces guilt, which undermines the wellbeing the tool exists to serve; framing it as ordinary and informative does not.

            Designing Toward Digital Self-Awareness and Wellbeing, 2025 · read it

            In Miru: no red, no target missed, no word like “productive”. Looking back sets two numbers beside each other and says nothing about them.

            A prompt that passes judgement produces the opposite behaviour

            When people feel their freedom is being constrained, they act to restore it — sometimes by deliberately doing the thing being discouraged. Autonomy-supportive wording, which offers a choice rather than a verdict, avoids this; moralising wording invites it.

            Reactance research, Brehm onward · read a recent test

            In Miru: the line above the dial states your own numbers and stops. There is no question, no adjective and no threshold that Miru chose for you.

            Most tools of this kind never reach the point of doing anything

            The stage model of personal informatics — preparation, collection, integration, reflection, action — finds that barriers cascade forward, and that most systems stop at reflection. Separately, plans in an if-then form have a medium-to-large effect on actually getting started, where an intention on its own routinely fails.

            Li, Dey & Forlizzi, CHI 2010 · read it — and the if-then planning literature · read it

            In Miru: every panel in Looking back ends in a door back to the settings that change something.

            What Miru cannot tell you
            Miru collects nothing and sends nothing, which means it has no idea whether any of this works for you in particular. There is no experiment running here, no cohort, and no measurement of your response. The evidence above is about people in general; you are the only one who can judge the rest.

            2. The principle: no data collection

            Miru is built so that every feature works entirely locally in your browser. The extension transmits no data to the developer or any third party, operates no servers, uses no analytics, tracking, or advertising services, loads nothing from the internet (fonts are bundled), and sets no cookies. The developer never has access to your data. All processing described below happens exclusively on your device.

            3. Locally stored data

            • Settings — your configuration (your chosen places and their posture — breathe, calm, or block — breathing pattern, night hours, theme). Stored in chrome.storage.sync (see section 4) until you change or delete them, or uninstall.
            • Usage times — time spent per site (domain and seconds) so the popup can show you your day and the "Looking back" section the past seven days. Stored in chrome.storage.local (this device only) for a maximum of 14 days, then deleted automatically.
            • Session state — a running focus session, a pending breath reminder, a running stay in a calmed site (domain, the length you chose, its end time, and the id of the tab concerned), a daily counter for brief unblocks of blocked sites ("stay five minutes"), and a technical note (domain name only) if calming a site no longer takes effect. Stored locally until it expires or is resolved.
            • Named lengths — the stays you name at the door of a calmed site: per day and site, the total minutes named and how many times you named one. This is what the "Looking back" section is built from; nothing further is measured. Stored in chrome.storage.local (this device only) for a maximum of 14 days, then deleted automatically.

            Detecting calls and fullscreen playback.
